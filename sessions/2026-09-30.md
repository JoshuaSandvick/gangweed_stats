### How to read this

- **The band** is Champion 2, one rank above this team's peak. Its numbers are 200 ranked 3v3 games measured with this same code, so a comparison is like for like — same playlist, same code, one rung up. Its games come from a fixed window of earlier months, which the appendix names, so a patch or meta change since then is not accounted for. Sitting below the band is not proof of a mistake: it says the lobbies above are doing something different.
- **Quarters.** "Best quarter" means the number is past the 25th percentile of the band in the good direction, "worst quarter" past the 75th in the bad one. A metric is judged this way only when the good direction is known *and* the measurement has been checked against an independent implementation; the rest are reported, and a grey flag means one is worth a look rather than good or bad.
- **Where the directions come from.** Some started as this project's own reasoning (less time without boost, less creeping ahead as last man) and some as the team's beliefs. The rank gradient below now decides every one of them: where stronger lobbies go the other way, the data wins, and a metric with no direction of its own takes the one the lobbies show.
- **The rank gradient.** The team decided on 2026-09-28 that more speed, supersonic time, powerslides, demolitions and touches is better. Those are beliefs, so each is tested against the same measurements taken in Champion 1, Champion 2 and Champion 3 lobbies, and across the nine ranks from Diamond 1 to Grand Champion 3: a direction counts when the metric moves with rank consistently enough to be statistically significant (a trend test on about 200 games a rank, 5% family-wise error, Holm-corrected). Significant means consistent, not large. Backed, and so judged: creeping ahead of the ball as last man (measured across Diamond 1–Grand Champion 3 lobbies), average speed (measured across Champion 1–3 lobbies and across Diamond 1–Grand Champion 3), time supersonic (measured across Champion 1–3 lobbies and across Diamond 1–Grand Champion 3), powerslides per game (measured across Champion 1–3 lobbies and across Diamond 1–Grand Champion 3), demolitions per game (measured across Diamond 1–Grand Champion 3 lobbies), touches per game (measured across Champion 1–3 lobbies and across Diamond 1–Grand Champion 3). Reversed, because stronger lobbies go the other way and the data decides: time at zero boost, longest spell without boost, boost droughts per game. An association across lobbies is not a cause: each band is a pool of lobbies where everyone was one rank, so it says what stronger lobbies do, not what would move a player up. A direction measured this way still needs the measurement itself to be checked outside this project before it can be called good or bad.
- **Your own reference** is your 11 games since the 2026 cutoff, and it is used only where you have enough of them for it to mean anything.
- **uu** are Unreal Units, the game's own unit of distance. The pitch is 10,240 uu from goal line to goal line (about 102 m), so treat 1 uu as roughly 1 cm and 100 uu as a metre. The body converts distances and speeds; the appendix keeps the game's own units.
- **Speeds.** 2,200 uu/s is supersonic, the fastest a car goes. The recovery numbers use 1,200 uu/s (43 km/h), a bit over half of that; 500 uu/s is close to stopped.
- **Time to get back up to speed** counts one episode: it starts when a car that was moving drops to 500 uu/s or less, and ends when it is back over 1,200 uu/s (43 km/h). It is how long someone was out of the play. **Times slowed to a near stop** is how many of those episodes there were. Neither is called good or bad: an episode is any drop to near-stopped and back, not recovery after a challenge, and a last man shadowing the ball slows down on purpose. A grey flag on them is a number to look at, never advice.
- **Last man** is the deepest of the three of you — the one back. **Creeping ahead as last man** is being ahead of the ball while you are that player and the ball is in your own half, the one situation where being ahead of the ball is a mistake.
- **✓** means the number was compared with ballchasing, an independent replay site, on a batch of games that had nothing to do with how the measurement was built — and ours matched. Numbers without ✓ are measured the same way but have not been checked outside this project.
- **Rotation exit** is crossing back out of the attacking half into your own half. **Through the ball's lane** means you came back through the line the ball is on instead of wide of it — the version where you can be hit or blocked on your way back.
- **Sharing a lane** is being within 400 uu (about 4 m) of a teammate *sideways* while in the attacking half: the measurable half of "two of you in the same place". Whether it is wrong depends on who should have been where, so it is reported as a measurement.
- **Big and small pads** are the boost pads each player took, split by which pad the car was nearest when it took one: the six 100-boost pads in the corners and on the side walls, and the twenty-eight 12-boost pads. A pickup counts only when the car's boost went up. Both match ballchasing's counts (✓).
- **Powerslides** count the handbrake held while the car is on the floor. Many players use the same button for air roll, so presses in the air are not slides; each stretch on the floor is one (✓).
- **Time in your own third / their third** is where the car was, the pitch cut into three equal lengths. The few seconds after a goal, when the cars have exploded and are waiting to reappear, are left out. Both match ballchasing (✓).
- **Touches** are read from the ball's own motion — every contact, not only the ones the game reports — and credited to the car that struck it: the ball takes the direction of the strike, so the striker is the car moving the way the ball has just started moving. That agrees with the game's own record 95.7% of the time, and where this game build reports its own per-player count the report quotes that instead. **Under pressure** means an opponent was within 600 uu (about 6 m) of the ball when you touched it. The replay cannot say who won the 50.
- **Your successful sessions.** A session is a night — games less than four hours apart — and it counts when you win more than 75% of them over at least 5 games — your decision, recorded 2026-09-28, with the minimum-games floor added by this project so a short night that went well is not a 100% session. When one exists, the report compares this night with the median of every game played in those sessions — the wins and the losses alike. Until then there is no such section, because a section that says "none yet" every night is noise.
